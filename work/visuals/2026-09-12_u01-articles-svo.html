This is the first unit — nothing above this has been taught yet, so nothing is repair. After this page you can write a simple, complete English sentence describing a UI element, with an article in the right slot most of the time. You cannot yet: talk about plurals or general categories without an article (U03), ask a question or say "not" correctly (U07), or use the technical/generic "the" of documentation (U08).

1 The whole system

English has exactly four articles. This unit teaches three of them — the fourth (zero article) is named in beat 6 and taught in U03. For a singular, countable noun, the choice is one two-step decision:

StepQuestionAnswerFormExample
1 Is this a specific thing the reader/listener can already identify — mentioned before, unique, or obvious from context? Yes the The button you clicked (the one we both know)
1 (same question) No — new, one of many, first mention go to step 2 —
2 What is the first sound of the word right after the article? Consonant sound a a team, a user (u sounds like "yoo" — a consonant sound)
2 (same question) Vowel sound an an interface, an element

Step 2 runs on sound, never spelling — this is what makes "a user" and "an hour" both correct at once.

2 The load

2 facts to memorise, not 15 — this is nothing like a gender list.
  1. New / one-of-many → a or an, picked by the sound right after it.
  2. Already known / specific / unique → the.
None of today's 15 nouns need their own memorised fact — no English noun "has" a fixed article the way a Romanian noun has a gender. The article is chosen fresh every time from context, not stored with the word.

3 The delta

Russian and Ukrainian have no article system at all — not "a different one", a total absence (docs/reference/transfer.md § Morphology traps). «Я открыл дизайн файл» is a complete, correct Russian sentence with no article-shaped gap in it anywhere — so there is nothing to translate an article *from*. Per this learner's ranking (Russian first, Ukrainian offers the same gap here, so one anchor is enough — never stack).
The trap: omission, not misselection. Because the category doesn't exist in either held language, the far more likely error for this learner is leaving the article out entirely (ART-MISS: ✗ "I opened design file") rather than picking the wrong one (ART-CHOICE: ✗ "I sent a feedback"). Expect ART-MISS to dominate the graded check.

4 Worked examples

A designer opens a new project.

Дизайнер открывает новый проект.

"Project" is new here, one of many possible projects, and "project" starts with a consonant sound → a. Notice Russian has nothing in this slot at all.

The designer is testing the layout now.

Дизайнер сейчас тестирует этот макет.

"The layout" — one specific layout both speaker and listener already have in mind (step 1 = yes) → the, regardless of its starting sound.

This is an interface, and a user is testing it.

Это интерфейс, и пользователь его тестирует.

"Interface" starts with the vowel sound /ɪ/ → an. "User" starts with the letter u but the sound "yoo" (a consonant sound) → a, not "an" — the step-2 rule runs on sound, and this is exactly the row it exists to get right.

5 First contact only

  • Zero article (∅) — plurals and uncountable nouns in the general case ("∅ Buttons should be consistent", "∅ Feedback matters") — the fourth form of the system above. → U03 (T-0005).
  • Irregular plurals (mouse/mice) — not needed yet, every noun taught today stays singular. → U03 (T-0008).
  • Generic/technical "the" in documentation register ("the user clicks the button" as a generic instruction, not a specific one) — a subtler use of the same word taught above. → U08 (T-0006).

The step-1/step-2 procedure above is complete for singular countable nouns — these three items are separate branches of the wider article system, not holes in what was just taught.

6 Your turn

1. I opened ___ project yesterday, and now ___ project is almost finished.
a project … the project — first mention (new, one of many) → a; second mention (now the specific one you both mean) → the.
2. She is building ___ prototype for ___ new feature. (prototype — new word, starts with the consonant sound "pr")
a prototype … a new feature — both new, both one-of-many, both start with consonant sounds → a twice.
3. Novel word — you have never seen it before: wireframe. "___ wireframe is a common tool in this project."
A wireframe — new/first mention, and "wireframe" starts with the consonant sound /w/ → a. Run the same two-step procedure on any word you have never met, even one this page never defines.

7 Carry away

Known already → the. New/one-of-many → a/an, chosen by the sound you say, not the letter you see.

Stress-tested against the hardest row in the table above: "user" starts with the letter u but the sound "yoo" — this line still gives a user, correctly, because it names sound and never spelling. A rule that said "vowel letter → an" would fail on this exact word.
